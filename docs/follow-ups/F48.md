title: "Persisted collections grow without an entry cap; trim in partialize as routstrStore does"
priority: medium
needs: decision
---

Persisted collections grow without an entry cap in 13 stores — `ownedMediaStore` (`byBlob`, 0.96), `transactionLocationStore`, `swapTransactionsStore`, `transactionDistributionStore`, `transactionAnnotationStore`, `mintStore`, `themeStore`, `ctaStore`, `nostrSocialStore`, `wallpaperStore`, `nip46ConnectionsStore`, `dataMigrations`. They cap key length and inner arrays (`z.string().max(600)`, `.max(2000)`) but not the number of entries, so the blob grows linearly with use forever — slower hydration and larger AsyncStorage, though `tolerantRecord` means it degrades rather than failing parse. `routstrStore` already solved this: it trims in `partialize` against named ceilings, and its comment records why — 1,024 sessions was a schema `.max()` with no trimming, so session 1,025 failed parse and `createMergeWithSchema`, being all-or-nothing, discarded the entire store. The pattern exists and was not propagated. Flagged by `conventions-state/persist-unbounded-collection` (14).


**Looked at on 2026-10-12.** The thirteen stores are three different things, and only one of them can be given a
ceiling without asking:

- **Already bounded by what their keys can be**, so they need nothing: `themeStore` (one wallpaper per unit),
  `ctaStore` (one entry per prompt the app defines), `mintStore` (one entry per payment method or mint the person
  trusts), `dataMigrations` (a level number).
- **Caches, already trimmed:** `nostrSocialStore` caps its engagement and zap maps by recency
  (`MAX_ENGAGEMENT_ENTRIES`, `MAX_ZAPPED_ENTRIES`). Its follow list is the person's own and is bounded by how many
  people they follow.
- **Things the person made, one entry per payment or upload:** `transactionAnnotationStore` (notes and links on a
  payment), `transactionLocationStore` (where a payment was made), `transactionDistributionStore` (how a payment
  was split), `swapTransactionsStore` (swap history), `ownedMediaStore` (what they uploaded, needed to delete it
  later), `nip46ConnectionsStore` (apps allowed to sign), `wallpaperStore` (what they downloaded). An entry is
  roughly 100 to 500 bytes, so ten thousand payments is a few megabytes read at every start. A ceiling here means
  deleting the oldest of something the person made, which the persistence rules forbid without the owner saying so.

**Question for the owner.** For the per-payment collections, what should happen as they grow?

1. **Keep everything and move it out of the start-up path (recommended).** Notes, locations, splits and swap
   history move from the saved blob into the wallet's database, keyed by payment, and are read when a payment is
   opened. Nothing is ever dropped and start-up stops depending on how many payments there are. It is a migration:
   a new saved layout that an older build cannot read back, so it needs its own release step.
2. **Keep the newest and drop the rest.** Each collection keeps its newest 5,000 entries. A person with more
   payments than that loses the note, place or split on the oldest ones, silently. Small change, no migration.
3. **Leave them as they are.** No ceiling. Start-up slows for a person with tens of thousands of payments, and
   nothing is lost. This entry is then closed as decided.

## Done when

Each persisted collection has an entry ceiling and a trim in `partialize`, with the eviction order chosen per store; ceilings shared by the schema and the trim as `routstrStore` does; existing oversized blobs trimmed on migration rather than rejected
